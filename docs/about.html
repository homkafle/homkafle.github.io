My name is Hom Kafle. I work in information security, but that is only one small doorway into who I am.

At the center of my life, I am trying to become a person who does the right thing when it is easy, when it is hard, and especially when no one is watching. That matters to me more than titles, roles, or the public version of success. I care about integrity. I care about kindness. I care about whether the work I do leaves people, systems, and the world a little safer than I found them.

I also love nature. Trees, rivers, mountains, open sky, rain, soil, and silence remind me that life is older and wiser than our plans. Nature has a way of returning me to scale. It shows me that everything participates in something larger: roots sharing ground, clouds becoming water, decay becoming life again. Nothing is truly isolated.

What I Am Learning

My view of God, self, and existence is changing. I do not feel the need to turn that change into a fixed doctrine. I am simply noticing that the boundary between "me" and "everything else" is not as solid as I once thought.

I am slowly learning that we may all be expressions of one consciousness, appearing for a while through different minds, bodies, names, histories, and circumstances. The mind and body are precious instruments, but I no longer feel sure that they are the whole of who we are. They may be more like a window than the sky itself.

Some days I wonder about the unseen forces that move through us before we can explain them. A will appears. It asks for something. We follow it. As soon as one desire is met, another arrives. Then another. Work, routine, hunger, ambition, fear, comfort, recognition, distraction. So much of life can become a chain of impulses that we obey before we even know we have chosen.

I am learning to pause there. To ask: who is aware of this will? Who is watching the thought arrive? Who notices the body move, the emotion rise, the story form, the next desire appear?

I do not have final answers. I am not trying to sound complete. I am more interested in being honest about the questions. The older I get, the more I feel that wisdom begins when certainty becomes softer.

Why Security Still Matters To Me

Professionally, I have spent more than a decade in offensive and defensive security, with a focus on application and product security. I have worked on code review, threat modeling, vulnerability validation, secure design, and the long practical work of helping teams reduce risk.

But security is not just technical to me. At its best, security is a form of care. It is the practice of protecting trust before trust is broken. It asks us to think about harm before harm happens. It asks us to see how one small weakness can affect many people we may never meet.

That is why I keep writing. This blog is where I try to connect the practical world of cybersecurity with the deeper questions that keep returning to me: What is responsible power? What does it mean to build safely? How do we use intelligence, human or artificial, without losing humility? How do we protect systems while remembering the living world those systems are meant to serve?

I am still becoming. That is the most honest thing I can say. I am a security engineer, a student of life, a lover of nature, and a human being trying to wake up a little more each day.
